This guide shows how voipred.dev implements the framework of Wang, Wang, Van Calster, van Geloven, Steyerberg and Wynants, Population-level decision curve analysis may mislead the evaluation of prediction model usefulness under subgroup utility heterogeneity. It is meant for collaborators: it reproduces the two examples of the paper, applies the framework to the GUSTO-I data, and goes through the options. Only the methods of the paper are implemented.

2 The idea

Net benefit is expressed in units of \(a - c\), the incremental utility of a true positive relative to a false negative (written \(k\)). Within a homogeneous population, the difference in utility between two strategies is \(\Delta U = k \, \Delta NB\), so net benefit and utility always favour the same strategy. With subgroups \(i = 1, \dots, m\) of sizes \(n_i\), the population difference in net benefit is the size-weighted sum of the subgroup differences, whereas the population difference in utility also weights each subgroup by its \(k_i\): \[\Delta NB \propto \sum_i n_i \, \Delta NB_i, \qquad \Delta U \propto \sum_i n_i \, k_i \, \Delta NB_i .\] When the \(k_i\) differ, the two can have opposite signs: population net benefit then favours one strategy and population utility the other. This is possible only when the subgroup \(\Delta NB_i\) do not all have the same sign. For two subgroups, the combinations \((\Delta NB_1, \Delta NB_2)\) for which this happens form the inconsistency region, between the lines \(y = -(n_1/n_2)\,x\) and \(y = -(n_1 k_1)/(n_2 k_2)\,x\), and the K-value \[K = -\frac{n_2 \, \Delta NB_2}{n_1 \, \Delta NB_1}\] is the critical ratio \(k_1/k_2\): when \(k_1 > k_2\), population net benefit and utility favour different strategies if \(k_1/k_2 > K\) (and if \(k_1/k_2 < K\) when \(k_1 < k_2\)).

The framework of the paper, and where it is in voipred.dev:

Step In the paper In voipred.dev
1 Decision curve analysis within subgroups nb_val(..., subgroup = )
2 Do the subgroup \(\Delta NB\) have the same sign? If so, differences in \(k\) cannot change the population conclusion summary(res): column signs
3 If not: is \((\Delta NB_1, \Delta NB_2)\) in the inconsistency region (with external evidence on \(k\), e.g. the relative risk reductions of the treatment for a prognostic model), or what is the K-value? With more than two subgroups: values or ranges of the \(k_i\) summary(res, k = ): columns K, delta_U and consistent; plot(res, type = "region")
The K-value from published results k_value()

3 The motivating example of the paper

Two subgroups of equal size, each with a prevalence of 50%, at a threshold of 0.0625 (Tables 1 and 2 of the paper; here 100 patients per subgroup, which gives the same proportions). The model has a specificity of 98% in both and a sensitivity of 90% in group 1 and 98% in group 2. The utilities give \(a_1 - c_1 = 60\) and \(a_2 - c_2 = 45\).

make_group <- function(tp, fp, fn, tn, group)
  data.frame(y = c(rep(1, tp), rep(0, fp), rep(1, fn), rep(0, tn)),
             p = c(rep(0.9, tp + fp), rep(0.01, fn + tn)),
             group = group)
d <- rbind(make_group(45, 1, 5, 49, "Group 1"),
           make_group(49, 1, 1, 49, "Group 2"))
res_m <- nb_val(data = d,
                predicted_risk = "p",
                outcome = "y",
                thresholds = 0.0625,
                label = "Model",
                subgroup = "group")
res_m

The subgroup \(\Delta NB\) against treat all have opposite signs (Step 2), the K-value is 1.308, and with \(k_1 / k_2 = 60/45 = 1.333 > K\), population net benefit and population utility favour different strategies (consistent is FALSE): the utility-weighted difference delta_U is negative (treat all is better on the utility scale, as the population utilities of 183 and 182.98 in the paper show). The region plot shows the observed point inside the inconsistency region:

plot(res_m, type = "region", threshold = 0.0625, k = c(`Group 1` = 60, `Group 2` = 45))

4 The case study of the paper: SCORE2, from published results

The paper took the net benefit of SCORE2 at a 3% threshold in men and women from a published validation study, assuming equal proportions of men and women: \(\Delta NB\) against treat all was \(-0.00160\) in men and \(0.00345\) in women. Without patient data, k_value() does the calculation; the relative risk reductions of LDL-lowering therapy (0.28 in men, 0.15 in women) give the ratio of \(k\):

k_value(c(Men = -0.0016, Women = 0.00345))
#> Subgroup delta NB: Men -0.00160, Women 0.00345 (opposite signs)
#> Shares: Men 50%, Women 50%
#> K-value (k_Men / k_Women): 2.156
#> Population NB and population utility favour different strategies if k_Men / k_Women is beyond 2.156 (above it when k_Men > k_Women, below it when k_Men < k_Women).
k_value(c(Men = -0.0016, Women = 0.00345), k = c(Men = 0.28, Women = 0.15))
#> Subgroup delta NB: Men -0.00160, Women 0.00345 (opposite signs)
#> Shares: Men 50%, Women 50%
#> K-value (k_Men / k_Women): 2.156
#> Population NB and population utility favour different strategies if k_Men / k_Women is beyond 2.156 (above it when k_Men > k_Women, below it when k_Men < k_Women).
#> With k: k_Men / k_Women = 1.867, so population NB and population utility favour the same strategy.
k_value(c(Men = -0.0016, Women = 0.00345), k = c(Men = 0.28, Women = 0.12))
#> Subgroup delta NB: Men -0.00160, Women 0.00345 (opposite signs)
#> Shares: Men 50%, Women 50%
#> K-value (k_Men / k_Women): 2.156
#> Population NB and population utility favour different strategies if k_Men / k_Women is beyond 2.156 (above it when k_Men > k_Women, below it when k_Men < k_Women).
#> With k: k_Men / k_Women = 2.333, so population NB and population utility favour different strategies.

As in the paper: K = 2.156; the evidence-based ratio 1.867 is below it (the same strategy is favoured), and the hypothetical ratio 2.333 (a relative risk reduction of 0.12 in women) is above it.

5 Application: GUSTO-I by age group

We fit a logistic model for 30-day mortality on a random development sample of 5,000 patients of the GUSTO-I trial and validate it on 5,000 other patients, with two subgroups: patients younger than 65 years, and those 65 and over.

val <- gusto[i[5001:10000], ]
fit <- glm(day30 ~ age + Killip + sysbp + pulse + pmi + miloc + sex,
           family = binomial, data = dev)
val$p_full <- predict(fit, newdata = val, type = "response")

At 3% to 6%, the model is better than treat all in one age group and worse in the other: the signs are opposite, so Step 3 is needed there. At the other thresholds the signs agree, and differences in \(a - c\) between the age groups cannot change the conclusion.

Step 3. The K-values at these thresholds say how different \(k\) would have to be between the age groups for population net benefit and utility to disagree. Here they range from 0.0023 to 0.039: the conclusion would change only if \(k\) in patients 65 and over were 26 to 426 times that in younger patients, so the population conclusion is robust to plausible differences in \(a - c\) between the age groups. Giving the subgroups. subgroup takes a column name, several column names (their combinations, e.g. subgroup = c("sex", "age_group")), or a factor or vector with one subgroup per patient (e.g. subgroup = val$age >= 65). Every patient needs a subgroup, and numeric variables with many values must be cut into categories first. as.data.frame() has the net benefit of the whole sample ("All") and of each subgroup:

head(as.data.frame(res_g), 3)
#>   threshold model subgroup    n   NB_model     NB_all NB_none     delta_NB
#> 1      0.01  Full      All 5000 0.05851515 0.05838384       0 0.0001313131
#> 2      0.02  Full      All 5000 0.05233878 0.04877551       0 0.0035632653
#> 3      0.03  Full      All 5000 0.04652990 0.03896907       0 0.0075608247

Survival and competing-risk outcomes. The net benefit of each subgroup uses the same estimator as nb_val() (Kaplan-Meier, or cIPCW with the censoring weights of the whole sample). For these estimators, the population net benefit is not exactly the size-weighted sum of the subgroup net benefits, which the framework assumes; the checks use the size-weighted sum.

Not available with subgroups. Credible intervals (interval), and combining results with c(): give all the models to one call of nb_val().
